# Digger for iPhone and iPad

A companion to the Digger on your computer. Choose a seed, go through the picks, audition them in
YouTube's player, keep or pass, follow a pick's label (or artist, or a credited person) into a new
step, and come back. Digger on your computer stays the source of truth: every dig, step, keep,
pass and heard mark is read from and saved to its database, so the browser app and the phone see
the same dig.

This is the first pass: the dig journey on iPhone and iPad, and sending a dig to its own private
YouTube Music playlist (the trail's Send to YT Music, as in the browser app). Feeds, the map,
library scans and keys stay in the browser app for now.

## YouTube Music

The trail (pushed on a phone, the sidebar on a wide iPad) starts with the dig's YouTube Music
keys, and the Dig menu has the same send: **Send to YT Music** makes a private playlist of the
dig on your computer's YouTube Music login (every step's picks in order, passes left out),
**Open in YT Music** opens it in the YouTube Music app (in Safari when the app isn't installed),
and **Update** brings it up to date.

The login stays on the computer. When Digger there has none, **Connect YT Music** opens the
Connection screen, where **Log in from Chrome on your Mac** asks that Digger to read YouTube
Music's sign-in from Chrome on the Mac, as the browser app's Setup does. macOS asks on the Mac
to use "Chrome Safe Storage" first; click Allow there, so do it while you're at the Mac. The
cookies are read and kept on the Mac and only the login's state comes to the phone. Logging in
again keeps the Google account the saved login acts as. While a login works, the phone doesn't
offer one. Changing account stays in the browser app's Setup, by pasting request headers from that
account, which the phone doesn't offer since it would send cookies over the network.

## What you need

- A Mac with Xcode 26 (the iOS 26 SDK). The app targets iOS 17 and later; the devices it's
  designed for, iPhone 17 Pro Max and iPad mini (A17 Pro), run iOS 26.
- Digger 0.4 or later on the same Mac, or on any computer the phone can reach.
- For a physical iPhone or iPad: an Apple ID added in Xcode > Settings > Accounts (a free one
  works, with 7-day provisioning), and Developer Mode on the device (Settings > Privacy & Security).

## Build and run

The project is generated by [XcodeGen](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen) from `project.yml`,
and the generated `Digger.xcodeproj` is checked in, so Xcode can open it straight away:

```bash
open ios/Digger.xcodeproj           # then pick the Digger scheme and an iPhone or iPad simulator
```

After changing `project.yml`, regenerate it: `brew install xcodegen && cd ios && xcodegen`.

From the command line, with [MobileBuildMCP](https://github.com/getsentry/MobileBuildMCP)
(`brew tap getsentry/xcodebuildmcp && brew install mobilebuildmcp`):

```bash
mobilebuildmcp simulator build-and-run --project-path ios/Digger.xcodeproj --scheme Digger \
  --simulator-name "iPhone 17 Pro Max"
mobilebuildmcp simulator list                       # the simulator ids for screenshot
mobilebuildmcp simulator screenshot --simulator-id <id>
mobilebuildmcp simulator test --project-path ios/Digger.xcodeproj --scheme Digger \
  --simulator-name "iPad mini (A17 Pro)"
```

The repo carries the MobileBuildMCP CLI skill (`.claude/skills/mobilebuildmcp-cli`) and Axiom's
`axiom-design`, `axiom-swiftui` and `axiom-audit-ux-flow` skills (`skills-lock.json`; refresh them
with `npx skills add CharlesWiltgen/Axiom`).

## Signing

The Simulator needs no signing. For a device, put your team in `ios/Config/Signing.local.xcconfig`
(git ignores it):

```
DEVELOPMENT_TEAM = ABCDE12345
PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER = com.yourname.cosinedigger
```

A free Apple ID can't use a bundle identifier someone else registered, so change it if Xcode says
so. The YouTube player identifies itself with whatever identifier you build with.

## Point the app at your Digger

A phone can't reach Digger at 127.0.0.1: on the phone, that's the phone. Companion mode listens on
your network and lets a device in only with a pairing token.

1. Stop a Digger you started with `cosine-digger start` (`cosine-digger stop`), then run
   ```bash
   cosine-digger serve --companion
   ```
   It prints a pairing link, the addresses this computer has (its Bonjour name, its LAN
   addresses, and its tailnet name when Tailscale is running) and the token. The app tries them
   all at once and keeps the first that answers, so the same pairing works at home and, over
   Tailscale, away from it. `cosine-digger companion --copy` prints them again and copies the
   link, so with Universal Clipboard the phone can paste it.
2. In the app, press **Paste**. Or AirDrop the link to the phone and open it, or type the address
   and token under **Type the address and token**.
3. Allow **Local Network** when iOS asks. If you said no, turn it on in Settings > Privacy &
   Security > Local Network > Digger.

The Simulator runs on the Mac, so there the address is `http://127.0.0.1:8765`, with the same
token. To type a Tailscale address by hand, use the Mac's tailnet name or 100.x address; the app
allows plain HTTP to `.ts.net` names (Tailscale's WireGuard tunnel encrypts it).

The token is the only thing that lets a device in (Host and Origin checks never authenticated
anyone), so anyone with it on your network can dig, keep and pass as you. A paired device can only
search, dig, read steps and jobs, keep, pass, mark heard, send a dig to its YouTube Music
playlist and ask the computer to log in to YouTube Music from the computer's Chrome; keys, pasted
logins, settings, scans, feeds and other exports refuse it. So someone holding the token can also
make playlists of your digs in your YouTube Music account, and make the Mac show macOS's Chrome
Safe Storage prompt (if you ever chose Always Allow there, Digger reads Chrome's sign-in without
asking). `cosine-digger companion --rotate` makes a new token, which a Digger already
running uses straight away; paired devices then have to pair again. When a pairing link is opened on
a device that's already paired, the app asks before replacing that pairing, since any app or web
page can open a link.

## Try it without keys

Digger's fixture mode answers from recorded, deterministic providers, so you can try the whole
journey in the Simulator without cosine.club or Discogs keys. It only binds to 127.0.0.1, so it
works with the Simulator, not a phone:

```bash
COSINE_DIGGER_TEST_MODE=1 COSINE_DIGGER_FIXTURE=slice-v1 \
COSINE_API_TOKEN=fixture-key DISCOGS_TOKEN=fixture-discogs \
COSINE_DIGGER_HOME="$(mktemp -d)" uv run cosine-digger serve --companion \
  --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8799 --no-scheduler
```

Both keys are stand-ins the fixtures accept; without the Discogs one, label and people moves say
they need a Discogs token. A label or person step reads the fixture Discogs at the real pace
(about a request a second), so its picks arrive in about 12 s and its record facts by about 25 s.

Pair with `http://127.0.0.1:8799` and the token it prints, then search for `Seed A`. Fixture
tracks are synthetic: only the first pick (YouTube's "Me at the zoo") is a real video, so the others
show how the app handles a video YouTube won't play.

With no Digger at all, launch the app with `-DiggerDemo` (Xcode: Product > Scheme > Edit Scheme >
Run > Arguments) to read recorded answers instead. Add `-DiggerDemoEmpty`, `-DiggerDemoOffline` or
`-DiggerDemoSlow` to see the empty, unreachable and loading states, and `-DiggerDemoYTDisconnected`
to start with YouTube Music not connected. The UI tests use this mode.

## Tests

```bash
cd ios/DiggerKit && swift test        # the shared core; runs on macOS and Linux
node ios/Tools/player-page-test.js    # the YouTube player page's script, against a fake IFrame API
```

The core's tests cover the recovery paths as well as the journey: a pairing link that doesn't work
never undoes one that does, a new pairing wins over a reconnect still waiting on the old token,
"can't reach Digger" clears on the next answer, a refused token pauses the player, an unreadable
dig list says why, and a video YouTube refuses moves the queue on only when the queue got there
by itself. Keeps and passes run against a stand-in that keeps revisions the way Digger does: a
double tap is keep then clear, Undo works twice in a row, an Undo that can't reach Digger stays
available, and a change made elsewhere meanwhile is refused rather than overwritten.

`LiveJourneyTests` also run the journey (pair, seed, keep, branch into the label, return, undo,
a retried request, a stale keep) against a running Digger when you set `DIGGER_LIVE_URL` and
`DIGGER_LIVE_TOKEN`, for example against the fixture server above:

```bash
DIGGER_LIVE_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8799 DIGGER_LIVE_TOKEN=<the token serve printed> swift test
```

Add `DIGGER_LIVE_FIXTURE=1` against the fixture server to also send a dig to YouTube Music, whose
playlist is a stand-in there; against your real Digger it would make one in your account.

The Digger scheme's test action runs these and the UI tests (`DiggerUITests`, on the demo Digger).
The backend's side is `tests/test_companion.py` (`uv run pytest tests/test_companion.py`).

## How it's put together

- `DiggerKit/` is a Swift package with no UI: Digger's wire models, the API client (token on every
  request; requests that create steps carry an Idempotency-Key and are retried with the same key),
  `DigSession` (the paired Digger, the open dig, its steps, selection and scroll position per step,
  keeps and passes with revisions and Undo, re-reading running steps) and `Playback` (what's
  playing, kept apart from what's selected).
- `Digger/` is the SwiftUI app. One `DigSession` drives two compositions: on iPhone and narrow
  iPad windows, the player stays on top of a navigation stack (step, pick details, trail); on wider
  iPad windows, a split view shows the trail, the step, and the player above the selected pick's
  details. The composition follows the width as well as the size class: below 640 points the
  phone composition is used, and below 1000 points the trail tucks away. Switching between them
  keeps the step, the selection, the scroll position, what's playing, what's pushed, and a new dig
  half chosen.
- `Digger/Player/YouTubePlayer.swift` is YouTube's IFrame player in one long-lived web view, loaded
  from `https://<bundle id>` so YouTube sees the app's identity. It plays inline only, at least
  200 points tall, with nothing over it; no Picture in Picture, AirPlay or background audio. The app
  pauses it whenever it can't be seen (another app, the lock screen, Control Center, or a
  connection problem taking over the screen), and a track only starts from a tap. If YouTube's
  player doesn't start within 15 seconds, or iOS reclaims the web view, the player says so and
  the next tap on play loads it again where it was.
- `Digger/Views/Theme.swift` and `Skin.swift` are the web client's skin, drawn natively: the
  colors in `src/cosine_digger/web/static/app.css`, its three faces (Press Start 2P for titles,
  VT323 for the LCD and ranks, Silkscreen for keys and labels, bundled in `Resources/Fonts` under
  the SIL Open Font License), beveled keys that press in, LED mode keys, title strips, the green
  LCD, and ERR/WARN alerts. The app is dark only, like the web client. On iOS 26 it opts out of
  Liquid Glass (`UIDesignRequiresCompatibility` in the Info.plist) because the glass bars and
  button bubbles would cover the skin's own chrome; that key is Apple's temporary opt-out, so a
  later iOS will need the title strip drawn another way.
